TITLE: AeroSync Aircraft Swap Policy (Demo)
DOCUMENT ID: AS-SWAP-001

## Purpose

Defines when the resolution engine may propose swapping the aircraft
assigned to a flight, and what conditions a proposed swap must satisfy
before it can be presented for approval.

## When a Swap Should Be Considered

An aircraft swap should be proposed when:

- The originally assigned aircraft has a projected delay exceeding 90
  minutes due to an unresolved technical or rotation issue.
- A technical fault has been logged against the assigned aircraft that
  is not expected to clear before the flight's revised departure window.
- Swapping unlocks a materially shorter cascade — i.e. the freed-up
  aircraft can recover a later flight's schedule by more time than the
  swap itself costs.

## Swap Eligibility Conditions

A candidate aircraft may only be swapped onto a flight if ALL of the
following hold:

1. Same or greater seating capacity as originally scheduled, unless the
   load factor for the affected flight is below 70%, in which case a
   smaller aircraft may be used with duty-manager approval.
2. Valid maintenance status with no open deferred items that restrict the
   route or sector length in question.
3. The candidate aircraft's own schedule allows it to be released without
   creating a new disruption of equal or greater severity elsewhere in the
   network.
4. Crew already assigned hold a valid type rating for the candidate
   aircraft, or replacement crew with the correct rating are available
   within the delay tolerance.

## Swap Cost Evaluation

Every proposed swap must include an estimated "cascade cost" — the total
passenger-minutes of delay it is expected to introduce elsewhere in the
network, weighed against the passenger-minutes of delay it prevents on
the original flight. A swap should not be proposed if its cascade cost
exceeds the delay it avoids.

## Approval

Swaps that keep the aircraft within the same fleet type and route family
may be proposed for standard approval. Cross-fleet swaps (e.g. narrow-body
for wide-body) always require duty-manager sign-off regardless of
projected benefit, due to the downstream crew and gate implications.

## Documentation

Every swap proposal must record: original aircraft, candidate aircraft,
trigger reason, estimated cascade cost avoided, estimated cascade cost
introduced, and approval status.
